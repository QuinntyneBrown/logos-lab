What Augustine Might Say About Large Language Models

A church historian reads today's chatbots alongside the Confessions, and finds an old warning about memory, attention and the restless heart.

Dr. Lucía OrtegaPublished 29 Sep 2026Updated 6 Oct 2026

Save 23 comments

Corrected on 6 Oct 2026

An earlier version said Augustine wrote the Confessions in 387. He began them around 397. Thank you to the readers who pointed this out.

The article

Augustine never saw a computer. But few writers have thought harder about what it is to remember, to pay attention and to be distracted, and those are exactly the things large language models seem to do.

In Book X of the Confessions, he walks through the “fields and vast palaces of memory” and is astonished by what he finds there. Memory, for Augustine, is not a store of records. It is where the self meets God: “late have I loved you, beauty so old and so new.”

What a model “remembers”

A language model is trained on a vast body of text and learns which words tend to follow which. When it answers, it is not recalling an experience. It is predicting, very well, what a plausible answer would look like.1

That difference matters. The psalmist asks, Psalm 8:4 (NRSV), “what are human beings that you are mindful of them?” God's remembering is personal. A model's “memory” is statistical.

“A store of data is not a self. Augustine would have said that a self is a memory turned toward love.”

Attention, then and now

Augustine also knew distraction. Watching a lizard catch flies, he confesses, could pull him away from prayer. The garden in Milan where he heard a child's voice say tolle, lege, “take up and read”, is a story about attention redirected.

Our tools are built to hold attention. The question for the church is not whether to use them, but what they train our hearts to love.

Three questions to take with you

  • What do I hope a chatbot will remember for me, and what should I remember myself?
  • Where does my attention go when I reach for a screen?
  • How would I describe the difference between being known and being predicted?
