3.2 Representing, not resembling

Text lessonAbout 14 minutesDr. Abena Mensah

In the ancient Near East, a king set up statues of himself in the far corners of his land. The statue did not look much like him, and nobody expected it to. Its job was to say: this territory belongs to this ruler, and his will holds here.

Read against that background, Genesis 1:26–28 is less about what humans are made of and more about what humans are for. We are placed in the world to represent the care of the One who made it.

Why the difference matters for AI

If the image were a set of abilities, then any system with those abilities would have a claim to it. If the image is a vocation given by God, then it is not the kind of thing that can be built, however capable the building.

“The image is not a trait we possess; it is a calling we receive.”

Before Thursday's cohort conversation

  • Where in your own work do you represent someone else's care?
  • What would change if we described software as a tool we use in that calling, rather than a rival to it?
