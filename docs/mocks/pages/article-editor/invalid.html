What the image is not

When Genesis 1:27 (NRSV) says that God created humankind in his image, it does not hand us a list of features: speech, reason, creativity. The Hebrew word צֶלֶם (tselem) names a likeness set up to represent someone.1

That matters now, because the features are exactly what new software imitates best.

Representing, not resembling

In the ancient Near East, a king set up statues of himself in the far corners of his land. They did not look much like him. Their job was to say whose care held there.

“The image is not a trait we possess; it is a calling we receive.”

Three things a model cannot do

Heading 4 follows a heading 2. Use heading 3.

  • A model can describe compassion; it cannot be moved by it.
  • A model can produce a prayer; it cannot address God.
  • A model can be useful in our calling; it cannot receive one.

As Psalm 8:4–6 (NRSV) puts it, the question is not what we can do but who is mindful of us.

  1. J. Richard Middleton, The Liberating Image (Brazos, 2005), 25–29. ↩
