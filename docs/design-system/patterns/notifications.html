Notifications

Tell people what changed through the quietest channel that still reaches them in time.

Pattern
  • Overview
  • When to use
  • Structure
  • States
  • Responsive behaviour
  • Accessibility

Overview

There are four channels, from quietest to loudest. Choose by how urgent the news is and how long it stays true, and use the quietest one that works.

Inline messageA result next to the thing that changedSaved at 10:42 amOn the next change
ToastA short outcome of the person's own actionNorthwind savedAfter about 6 seconds; errors stay
Alert or bannerA condition that keeps affecting the page or accountYou're offlineWhen the condition ends
Notification centreNews from other people or the product, worth revisitingDaniel Reyes commented on Spring route optionsWhen read; history stays

Email and push follow the same words as the notification centre, and are for things people asked to hear about.

When to use

  • Toast: the person just did something on this page and it worked (or didn't). Brief, with at most one action, usually Undo or View.
  • Banner: a condition that affects everything on the page or in the account: offline, read-only mode, an unverified email, scheduled maintenance. Sits at the top of the page, above the header.
  • Alert: a condition that affects one region or form on the page.
  • Notification centre: something happened that the person didn't do themselves and may want to come back to: a comment, an invitation, a reminder.
  • Not for marketing, tips or anything a person didn't ask to hear.

Structure

Every notification follows the same order: what happened, then to what, then an optional action and a way to dismiss it.

States

  • Toast — info, success, warning, danger: the icon and its colour change; the words always say which. Success and info dismiss themselves after about 6 seconds (10 or more with an action); warnings and errors stay until dismissed.
  • Toast — stacked: at most three at once, newest at the bottom; older ones leave first. Group repeats: "3 notes archived", not three toasts.
  • Banner — persistent: some conditions can't be dismissed (offline, read-only); others can, and stay dismissed for that session.
  • Notification centre — unread, read, empty, failed: reading an item or Mark all as read removes the tint and the word New; the history stays. Empty says "You're all caught up".

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: toasts stretch across the bottom inside the page margins, above the safe area and above any bottom navigation. Banner text wraps; its action drops below the text.
  • 768px and up: toasts sit in the bottom-right corner at a capped width. Banners keep text and action on one line when they fit.
  • Notification centre: rows wrap long titles at every width; the time moves under the title when space runs out.

Accessibility

Live regions: keep one polite role="status" region on the page for toasts and add messages into it. Use role="alert" only for a new error. Never move focus to a notification.

Timing: auto-dismiss pauses while the toast is hovered or has focus; any toast with an action stays at least 10 seconds. Nothing important exists only inside a toast; the same action is available on the page.

Dismiss buttons name the message ("Dismiss: Note archived"), so a list of them makes sense.

Notification centre: it is a normal list, not a live region; don't re-announce history. Unread is said in words (a "New" badge or visually hidden text), not only by tint or a dot. The bell's accessible name includes the count ("Notifications, 3 unread").

Not colour alone: each variant has its own icon and its own words.

Content

  • Lead with the outcome, past tense, naming the object: "Northwind saved", "Note archived", "Invitation sent to Grace Liu".
  • Other people's actions start with the person: "Daniel Reyes commented on Spring route options".
  • Conditions say what is true and what still works: "You're offline. What you write stays on this page until you reconnect."
  • Actions are one or two words: Undo, View, Try again, Send it again.
  • Times are relative when recent ("9:12 am", "Yesterday, 6:00 pm") and dates after that ("Wed 7 Oct").
  • No exclamation marks, no "Oops", and never "Success!".
