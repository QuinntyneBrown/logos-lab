Feedback and loading

Show what is happening and what changed, close to where it happened, for as long as it matters.

Pattern
  • Overview
  • When to use
  • Structure
  • States
  • Responsive behaviour
  • Accessibility
  • Content
  • Do and don’t
  • Sources

Overview

Every action gets an answer, and the answer sits where the person is looking. Loading reserves the space that content will fill, so nothing jumps. Work in progress shows on the control that started it. Outcomes are quiet and specific: "Northwind saved", not "Success!". Failures keep everything in place and offer one way forward.

Pick the feedback by where the change happened and how long it matters:

What happenedUseLasts
A field or row changed on its ownInline messageUntil the next change
A page region is loadingSkeleton matching the final layoutUntil the content arrives
A button's action is runningSpinner inside the buttonUntil it finishes
A long task with known progressProgress bar with a countUntil it completes
A short action finishedToastAbout 6 seconds; errors stay
A condition affects the whole pageAlert or bannerWhile the condition lasts
A decision is needed before continuingDialogUntil the person decides

When to use

  • Loading content on first visit or navigation: a skeleton for anything that takes more than about 300ms. Show nothing for faster loads; a flash of grey is worse than a short wait.
  • Refreshing content that is already visible: keep it on screen and show a small inline status ("Updating…"). Never swap loaded content back to a skeleton.
  • Running an action: a spinner inside the pressed button, with a present-tense label.
  • Uploading, importing or syncing: a progress bar when the total is known; otherwise a spinner with a sentence.
  • Confirming an outcome: inline for a field-level save, a toast for a page-level action, the new page itself for a created object.

Structure

Skeleton, then content

A skeleton is a calm outline of the real layout: same cards, same rows, same gaps. It is decorative, so the region around it carries the busy state and an accessible name.

States

  • Idle: no feedback at all. Don't pre-announce what hasn't happened.
  • Pending: skeleton for a region, spinner for an action, progress for a known total. Everything around it stays usable.
  • Optimistic: for small, reversible changes (a star, a checkbox, a rename), show the result at once and confirm quietly. If the save fails, put the old value back and say so beside it.
  • Success: say what changed, in the past tense, naming the object. Offer Undo when the change can be reversed.
  • Failure: say what didn't happen, that nothing was lost, and one way forward. Errors never disappear on their own.

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: skeletons match the one-column layout, so reserve one card per row. Toasts span the bottom of the screen inside the page margins, above the safe area.
  • 768px: skeletons follow the two-column grid. Toasts move to the bottom-right corner, capped at the toast width and stacked with the newest at the bottom.
  • 1024px and up: reserve space for side columns too; a missing aside that appears later shifts the whole page.

A busy button keeps its width at every size; a spinner never replaces a label.

Accessibility

Busy regions: set aria-busy="true" on the region that is loading and give it a name ("Loading your projects"). Skeleton shapes are aria-hidden="true".

Announcements: outcomes go to a polite live region (role="status") that already exists on the page. Use role="alert" only for a new failure that needs attention. Never move focus to a toast.

Timing: a toast with an action stays at least 10 seconds and pauses while hovered or focused. Error toasts stay until dismissed. Never put the only route to an action inside a toast.

Progress: role="progressbar" with aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax and a label. An indeterminate bar drops aria-valuenow.

Motion: the shimmer, spinner and toast rise stop under prefers-reduced-motion; the static shapes and words still carry the meaning.

Colour: success and error are always said in words and shown with an icon, not by colour alone.

Content

  • Pending: present tense with an ellipsis: Saving…, Sending invitation…, Importing 42 of 120 pages.
  • Success: past tense, naming the object: Northwind saved. Invitation sent to Grace Liu.
  • Failure: what didn't happen, then reassurance, then the fix: "Invitation not sent. Your message is still in the form. Try again."
  • Undo is a verb on its own; never "Click here to undo".
  • No exclamation marks, no "Oops", no "Success!".
