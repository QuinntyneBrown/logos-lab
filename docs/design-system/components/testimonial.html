Testimonial and verse

Let a real person speak: a small quote card with a portrait, or one centred line set like a verse between sections.

Standard componentContrast verified

Overview

Two quiet ways to quote someone. The quote is a small surface card with a square portrait, the words and who said them; it sits beside a hero or in a row of three. The verse is one centred line in the light display face with a short sage rule above it; it marks a pause between two page sections, once per page at most. Neither is interactive. To show a person's profile use a person list; to show a comment in a thread use a comment.

Used in 1 mock state.

Anatomy

  1. Portrait.quote__photo, a 56px square photo with --radius-md; decorative (alt="").
  2. Words.quote__text in the body role, wrapped in curly quotation marks.
  3. Attribution.quote__by, a caption with name · role, organisation.
  4. Rule.verse__rule, a 40px hairline in --color-accent, decorative.
  5. Verse text.verse__text, light display face, 28px (36px from 1024px), at most 30 characters a line.
  6. Reference.verse__ref, an overline naming the speaker and the occasion.

Sizes

one content-driven size. The quote card fills its column; the verse is centred and capped at the verse measure (30 characters), stepping up from 28px to 36px at 1024px.

States

Both are passive content with no interactive states. A quote never links as a whole; if the speaker has a profile, link their name inside .quote__by and it follows the link states.

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: the quote keeps its portrait beside the words (56px column); long quotes wrap under the portrait's height. The verse wraps to four or five short lines and its top padding stays at the section gap.
  • 768px: a quote row still stacks, one card per line, each with its portrait beside the words.
  • 1024px and up: the verse text steps up to 36px; a quote row becomes three equal columns and each card puts its portrait above the words.

Theming

The quote card reads --color-bg-surface and --color-border-default; the verse sits directly on the page canvas. The rule reads --color-accent, sage in both themes. Portraits keep their own colours in dark mode; the card's border separates them from the canvas.

Accessibility

Role: a <blockquote> for each quote. The verse is a <section> with an aria-label that names the occasion, so it appears in the landmarks list only when it adds meaning; its rule is aria-hidden="true".

Focus: neither component takes focus. Any link inside the attribution uses the shared focus ring.

Labelling: the portrait uses alt="" because the speaker's name is in the attribution; never repeat the name in alt. Use real curly quotation marks so screen readers announce the words naturally.

Motion: none. The verse may use the page's scroll-driven .reveal drift, which is off under reduced motion.

Touch: no targets, apart from an optional name link in the attribution.

Content

  • Quote real people, with their permission, in their own words. Keep it under 25 words and specific: "Everything we agree on is in Harbour Notes by lunch", not "Great product!".
  • Attribution is name · role, organisation. Drop the organisation rather than invent one.
  • A verse is one sentence of encouragement or a principle the team lives by, under 20 words, with a reference that names the speaker and occasion: "Hannah Kowalski · Field Day 2025".
  • One verse per page. Never use a verse for marketing claims.

Code

Link tokens/tokens.css and assets/components.css, then use this markup. The data-state attribute only forces a state for review; production code uses real interaction and native attributes.

<blockquote class="quote">
  <img class="quote__photo" src="../assets/photos/grace-liu.jpg" width="56" height="56" alt="">
  <div>
    <p class="quote__text">“We stopped losing decisions in chat. Everything we agree on is in Harbour Notes by lunch.”</p>
    <p class="quote__by">Grace Liu · Designer, Northwind</p>
  </div>
</blockquote>
