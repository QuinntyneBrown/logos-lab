Prose

Set long-form pages such as privacy, terms and help articles in a calm, readable column with a clear heading rhythm.

Overview

Wrap any long-form text you do not control line by line — privacy policy, terms, help articles, an about page, rendered Markdown — in .prose. It styles plain h2, h3, p, ul, ol, strong and a elements, caps the column at --layout-narrow (44rem) and spaces every block evenly, so content authors never add classes. Use it under a page header. Do not use it for interface copy such as form help or card text; those components set their own type.

Kit component; mocks have not been written yet.

Anatomy

  1. Column.prose, a grid capped at 44rem with 20px between blocks.
  2. Section headingh2, light display face at 28px, with 24px extra space above.
  3. Subheadingh3 in the h3 role (22px sans, medium), with 16px extra space above.
  4. Paragraphp in the large body role (19px) and --color-fg-muted, wrapped with text-wrap: pretty.
  5. Listul or ol indented 24px, 8px between items.
  6. Emphasis and linksstrong returns to --color-fg-default; links keep the underlined link role.

Variants

Privacy

A policy section: heading, paragraph, bulleted list, subheading and a link.

What we collect

Logos Lab keeps only what it needs to run your workspace: your name, your email address, and the projects, notes and gatherings your team creates.

  • Account details you give us when you sign up.
  • Your content: what your team writes in projects such as Northwind and notebooks such as Harbour Notes.
  • Basic logs of sign-ins and errors, kept for 30 days so we can fix problems.

What we never do

We never sell your data, show you ads or read your notes to train anything. Read how we protect your workspace for the detail.

Terms of use

Last updated Friday 9 October 2026. These terms are written to be read; if anything is unclear, ask us.

1. Your account

You are responsible for the people you invite. Remove anyone who leaves the team from Settings › People.

2. Your content

What you write stays yours. You give Logos Lab permission to store it and show it to the people you share it with, and nothing more.

3. Ending your account

You can export everything and delete your workspace at any time. We remove your content within 30 days.

Prose · terms

Help article

Steps as an ordered list, with interface names in bold and a closing tip.

Invite someone to Harbour Notes

Anyone with edit access can invite people to a notebook. Invitations last seven days.

  1. Open Harbour Notes and choose Share at the top right.
  2. Type a name or email address, for example Daniel Reyes.
  3. Choose Can edit or Can read, then Send invitation.

If the invitation does not arrive

Ask them to check their spam folder, or copy the invitation link from Share › Pending and send it yourself.

About

A short introduction: one heading and two paragraphs.

Why we built Logos Lab

We are a small team who kept losing decisions in chat threads and meeting notes nobody could find. Northwind, Harbour Notes and Field Day were our own projects first.

Logos Lab is the calm place we wanted: fewer notifications, clearer notes, and more time for the work itself.

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: the column fills the width inside the page margins; headings wrap with text-wrap: balance and long links break anywhere rather than overflowing.
  • 768px: the column reaches its 44rem cap and sits at the start of the content area.
  • 1024px and up: unchanged; pair it with an aside or a sidebar navigation for long policies.

Theming

Paragraphs read --color-fg-muted, headings and strong read --color-fg-default, links read --color-fg-link. In dark mode every role shifts with the theme; no prose-specific colours exist.

Accessibility

Role: plain document semantics. Put the page's h1 in the page header, then h2 for sections and h3 inside them; never skip a level for size. Use ol for steps that happen in order and ul otherwise.

Focus: only links take focus, with the shared sage ring offset 3px.

Labelling: link text names the destination ("how we protect your workspace"), never "here". Interface names are in strong so they stand out without colour.

Motion: none.

Touch: links inside paragraphs use the large body line height (about 32px), which keeps adjacent lines' links apart; put important links on their own line.

Content

  • Write in plain words, second person, short sentences: "What you write stays yours."
  • Lead each section with the answer, then the detail.
  • Use real dates in full ("Friday 9 October 2026") and real interface names in bold: Settings › People.
  • Keep paragraphs to four sentences; split a long list into sections with h3.

Code

Link tokens/tokens.css and assets/components.css, then use this markup. The data-state attribute only forces a state for review; production code uses real interaction and native attributes.

<div class="prose">
  <h2>What we collect</h2>
  <p>Logos Lab keeps only what it needs to run your workspace: your name, your email address, and the projects, notes and gatherings your team creates.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Account details</strong> you give us when you sign up.</li>
    <li><strong>Your content:</strong> what your team writes in projects such as Northwind and notebooks such as Harbour Notes.</li>
    <li><strong>Basic logs</strong> of sign-ins and errors, kept for 30 days so we can fix problems.</li>
  </ul>
  <h3>What we never do</h3>

</div>
