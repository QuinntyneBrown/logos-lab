Toast

Confirm that something just happened without taking people away from their work. Raised, brief, and in the corner.

Core componentContrast verified

Overview

A toast confirms the result of something the person just did — Note saved, Invitation sent — and offers at most one follow-up action, such as Undo. It appears in the bottom corner, above everything, and leaves on its own. Use an alert when the message must stay on the page, an inline message when it belongs to one field or button, and a dialog when the person must decide something. Never use a toast for the only copy of important information.

Used in 1 mock state.

Anatomy

  1. Icona 20px line icon in the tone's icon colour; decorative.
  2. Titlethe outcome in a few words, in --text-label.
  3. Bodyoptional: one sentence of detail in muted text.
  4. Actionoptional: one text button, such as Undo or View.
  5. Dismissa 32px icon button, last in reading order and top right on screen.
  6. Region.toast-region, the fixed stack in the bottom corner that holds every toast.

Sizes

one size. A toast is 24rem wide in the desktop region and full width (less the page margin) on phones. It grows taller with its text; keep it to a title and one sentence.

States

A toast rises in over 420ms when it arrives (reduced motion: it simply appears). While the pointer is over it, or focus is inside it, the timer pauses. When it times out or is dismissed it is leaving: it fades before it is removed, and the toasts below close the gap. Danger toasts have no timer. The action and dismiss buttons follow the button states.

Responsive behaviour

Below 768px the region spans the bottom of the screen, 16px from each edge and above the safe area, and toasts take the full width. From 768px the region sits 24px from the bottom-right corner, 24rem wide. Toasts never cover the mobile bottom navigation; lift the region above it.

Theming

Toasts use --color-bg-surface-raised with --shadow-3 so they float above both themes; in dark mode the raised surface is a lighter charcoal than the page. Icons read the tone's *-icon role; danger also tints the border.

Accessibility

Role: keep one empty .toast-region in the page from the start, so new toasts inside it are announced. Each toast is role="status" (polite); a danger toast is role="alert" (assertive). Do not add aria-live to the region as well, or messages are read twice.

KeyEffect
Tab / Shift+TabReaches the action and the dismiss button in reading order; the timer pauses while focus is inside.
Enter, SpaceActivates the action or dismisses the toast.
EscapeDismisses the toast that has focus.

Focus: a toast never takes focus when it appears. If the person is on the dismiss button when it leaves, return focus to the control that caused it.

Labelling: the title and body are the message. Dismiss buttons say "Dismiss notification". Anything in a toast must also be reachable elsewhere (Undo in the menu, the message in Sent), because a toast may leave before someone can use it.

Motion: the 420ms rise and the fade on leaving disappear under reduced motion; timing is unchanged.

Touch: the action is 36px tall and the dismiss button keeps a 32px button inside a 44px target area. Give people at least 6 seconds (10 with an action) before a toast leaves — WCAG 2.2.1.

Content

The title is the outcome in the past tense and names the thing: Invitation sent to Grace Liu, Note moved to Archive, Comment not posted. Add a body only when it says something new: where to find it, what is safe, what to do. One action, named with a verb: Undo, View conversation, Try again. No "Success!" or "Oops", and no exclamation marks.

Code

Render toasts into one .toast-region that is always in the page. Each .toast has an icon, .toast__title, optional .toast__body and .toast__action, and an .icon-btn.toast__close last. Use role="status", or role="alert" for danger.
