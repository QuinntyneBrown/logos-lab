Alert and banner

Tell people something that stays true while they are on the page. Tinted, calm, specific, and always with a way forward.

Overview

An alert (.alert) sits in the page content and explains a condition that affects this screen: a form that did not send, a project that is read-only, a change that worked. A banner (.banner) runs across the top of the app for something that affects everything — planned maintenance, delayed messages. A strip (.strip) is a quiet sage band for one friendly sentence and one link. For a short confirmation that goes away, use a toast; for one line beside a field or button, use an inline message; for a whole screen that failed, use an error page.

Not used in the mocks yet.

Anatomy

  1. Icona 20px line icon in the tone's icon colour; decorative.
  2. Titleone short line in --text-h4 that says what happened.
  3. Bodyone or two sentences in the default text colour: why, and what happens next.
  4. Actionsoptional small buttons or links that resolve it.
  5. Dismissoptional icon button, only for alerts that do not need to stay.

Alert and banner · strip

Sizes

alerts fill their container and grow with their text. Banners span the full width and keep the page container inside. A strip is as wide as its column.

States

Alerts, banners and strips are passive messages, so they have one state. Their tone is chosen for what they say, not changed by interaction. A dismissible alert disappears when closed and does not return on the next visit; focus moves to the next heading or the main content. Actions inside follow the button and link states.

Responsive behaviour

At 360px an alert keeps its icon column and its actions wrap under the body; a banner's text takes the full line and its action and dismiss button drop below it. A strip stacks its sentence above the link. From 768px banner actions sit at the end of the line. Alerts never exceed the content column width.

Theming

Each tone pairs *-bg, *-border, *-fg and *-icon roles that all change in dark mode. Body text uses --color-fg-default so it stays readable on every tint; the strip reads the accent-subtle pair.

Accessibility

Role: an alert that is on the page when it loads needs no live role. One that appears after an action gets role="status" (info, success, warning) or role="alert" (danger), and must be inserted into a container that already exists so it is announced once. A banner inserted after load takes role="status". Never put role="alert" on content that is always present.

KeyEffect
Tab / Shift+TabMoves to the actions and the dismiss button in reading order.
Enter, SpaceActivates an action or dismisses the alert.

Focus: do not move focus to an alert, except after a failed form submit, where focus goes to the alert (with tabindex="-1") so people hear the problem first. Dismissing moves focus to the next logical element.

Labelling: the title and body are the message; the icon is aria-hidden. The dismiss button names what it closes: "Dismiss: new weekly digest".

Motion: none. Alerts appear in place without sliding; dismissing removes them instantly.

Touch: actions use btn--sm (36px) and the dismiss button keeps a 44px target.

Content

Lead with the outcome, then the reason, then the next step: We couldn't save your note · Your text is still here · Check your connection, then save again. Be specific — name the project, the person, the time (until 4:00 pm). Never blame ("You entered an invalid…"), never use "Error!" or "Oops", and keep the title under eight words. Success alerts say what changed; info alerts say why it matters.

Code

Use .alert with a tone modifier, an icon, .alert__title, .alert__body and optional .alert__actions; add an .icon-btn.alert__close last to make it dismissible. Banners wrap a .wrap.banner__inner; strips hold one <p> and one link.
