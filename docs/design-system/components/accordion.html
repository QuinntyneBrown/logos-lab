Accordion

Fold secondary detail under a short question or heading, so a long page stays calm until someone asks for more.

Standard componentContrast verifiedUnused in mocks

Overview

Use an accordion for answers and detail that only some people need: questions about a gathering, the fine print of a plan, optional settings. Each item is a native <details> element, so it works without script. Don't hide anything most people need to finish their task — put that on the page. Use tabs when the sections are peers of equal weight, and a dialog when the content needs a decision.

Not used in the mocks yet.

Anatomy

  1. Summarythe always-visible row: a question or heading in the label face, 44px tall at least.
  2. Leading iconoptional, decorative 20px line icon.
  3. Indicatorthe plus that becomes a minus when the item is open, drawn in --color-fg-subtle.
  4. Bodythe answer, in muted body text (.accordion__body).
  5. Hairlinea bottom rule that separates one item from the next.

Variants

Single

One item on its own, for a single piece of optional detail.

Several independent items. Any number can be open at once; this suits a list of questions people read in any order.

Exclusive

Items that share a name attribute open one at a time; opening one closes the others. Use it for steps or alternatives that people compare one by one.

Morning: demos

Ten-minute demos from each team, starting at 10:00 am.

Midday: lunch together

A long table, no agenda. Grace Liu is organising the food.

Afternoon: planning

We set three goals for the next quarter and write them in Harbour Notes.

Sizes

one size. The summary is at least --target-comfortable (44px) tall and grows with its text.

States

Hover turns the summary text sage; keyboard focus draws the 2px ring around the summary. Open swaps the plus for a minus and reveals the body. There is no disabled accordion: if the content is not available, leave the item out.

Responsive behaviour

Accordions are full width at every breakpoint. At 360px long questions wrap onto two lines and the indicator stays pinned to the right edge. On wide pages keep accordions inside the reading measure (--layout-narrow) so the indicator does not drift far from the question.

Theming

Every colour comes from a semantic role, so the dark theme needs no component-specific rules. A light specimen nested under a dark root resets its roles.

Accessibility

Role: the native <details> / <summary> disclosure: the summary is exposed as a button with its expanded state. It follows the APG disclosure pattern; use the full accordion pattern with heading-wrapped buttons only when the items must appear in the page outline.

KeyEffect
Tab / Shift+TabMoves between summaries and any links inside open bodies.
Enter, SpaceOpens or closes the focused item.

Focus: focus stays on the summary when the item opens; the body follows it in tab order.

Labelling: write the summary as a complete question or heading. Icons and counts are supplementary; the count badge is read as part of the summary text.

Motion: the body appears instantly; there is no height animation to wait for or to disable.

Touch: the whole summary row is the target, 44px tall at minimum.

Content

Write summaries as the question people actually ask: Can I export my notes?, not Export. Answer in the first sentence of the body, then add detail. Keep each body to a short paragraph or a list of three to five items; if it needs more, it needs its own page.

Do and don’t

Tokens

Accordions read the default, muted and accent text roles, the hairline border and the comfortable target height.

TokenLightDark
--border-width-hairline
--color-border-default
--space-3
--target-comfortable
--text-label
--text-h4
--color-fg-subtle
--color-fg-accent
--space-2
--color-fg-muted

Code

Use native <details class="accordion"> with a <summary> and one .accordion__body. Give related items the same name to make them exclusive. Add open to start an item expanded.

<details class="accordion"><summary>What should I bring to Field Day?</summary><div class="accordion__body"><p>Just yourself and a laptop if you are showing a demo. Lunch and coffee are covered.</p></div></details>
