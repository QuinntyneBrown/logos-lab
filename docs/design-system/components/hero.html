Hero

Open a landing or welcome page with one calm promise, one sage action and a small, human photograph.

Standard componentContrast verified

Overview

Use the hero once, at the top of a landing, welcome or event page, to say what the place is for in one sentence. It is the loudest type in the system — a light display face with one phrase turned sage — so it appears once per product surface, never inside an app screen. Signed-in screens open with a page header instead; a mid-page invitation is a grain panel.

Used in 1 mock state.

Anatomy

  1. Eyebrowoptional overline that names the audience or the occasion, with its short hairline.
  2. Titlethe page's one h1 in the display role, at most about 14 characters per line.
  3. Accent phraseone em inside the title, set in --color-fg-accent, never italic.
  4. Leadone or two sentences in the large body role, muted, within the reading measure.
  5. Actionsone primary lg button and, optionally, one quiet lg button with a trailing arrow.
  6. Asideoptional: a small square photograph with a two-part caption, and one short quote.

Variants

Promise

The full landing hero: promise, two actions, and an aside with a photograph and one quote. At 1024px and up the aside sits to the right and aligns to the bottom of the text.

Sizes

Two sizes, set by modifier rather than a size scale: the default hero uses the display role and roomy section padding (96px above at 1024px and up); hero--compact uses the h1 role and roughly two thirds of the padding, for event pages, welcome screens and heroes followed by stats. The photograph is capped at --size-photo-sm (18rem) at every size.

States

The hero itself is static; its actions carry the states. Hover deepens the primary pill and nudges the quiet button's arrow; keyboard focus draws the sage ring around the focused action. The photograph eases to 103% on pointer hover when motion is welcome.

Responsive behaviour

  • 360px: one column. Eyebrow, title, lead and actions stack; actions wrap onto two lines at full label width; the aside follows the text with the photo at its natural width and the quote below it.
  • 768px: still one column, with wider measure. The two actions sit side by side.
  • 1024px and up: two columns (1.6fr and 1fr) with an 80px gap, aligned to the bottom so the photo's caption lines up with the actions. hero--centred stays one column at every width.

The display size is fluid (clamp(2.75rem, 1.5rem + 5vw, 5.25rem)), so the title never needs a manual break.

Theming

Every colour reads a role: the title uses --color-fg-default, the accent phrase --color-fg-accent (sage 700 in light, sage 300 in dark), the lead --color-fg-muted. The photo frame shows --color-bg-subtle while the image loads, so a dark page never flashes a light square.

Accessibility

Role: a <section> labelled by its title (aria-labelledby). The title is the page's one <h1>. The photograph is a <figure> with a <figcaption>; the quote is a <blockquote>.

KeyEffect
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the actions in reading order: primary first, then quiet.
EnterFollows a link action; Enter or Space activates a button action.

Focus: the shared 2px --color-focus-ring, offset 3px, around each action. Nothing else in the hero takes focus.

Labelling: the em is visual emphasis only and is read as part of the title. The scene photo has a descriptive alt; the quote portrait uses alt="" because the name sits beside it.

Motion: the photo's 103% scale and the arrow nudge run only under prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference; with reduced motion the hero is completely still.

Touch: lg actions are 52px tall; wrapped actions keep a 12px gap so neighbouring targets never touch.

Content

  • Write the title as a promise in plain words, about 6 to 10 words: "Do your best work, alongside people you trust." Put the benefit, not the product name, in the accent phrase.
  • The lead says who it is for and what they get, in one or two sentences under 30 words.
  • The primary action is a verb and an object: Start a project, Save my seat. The quiet action invites a look: See how it works.
  • Captions are two facts, place and time: "Planning Field Day · Fri, 9:30 am".

Code

Use .hero with a text column and, optionally, .hero__aside. Add hero--centred for a single centred column and hero--compact for the smaller title and padding. Keep exactly one em in the title.
