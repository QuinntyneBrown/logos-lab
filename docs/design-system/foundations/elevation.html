Elevation

Hairlines before boxes; four warm, very soft shadows; named layers for everything that floats.

Foundation
  • Principles
  • Specimens
  • Tokens
  • Usage
  • Sources

Principles

Most surfaces are separated by a hairline and a small change of tone, not by a shadow. Shadows are warm (tinted with --shadow-color, a brown in light mode) and very soft, with long, low blur. They signal that something floats:

ShadowUse
--shadow-1resting items on a tinted panel (panel list items)
--shadow-2hover lift on cards and areas
--shadow-3menus, popovers, toasts, hovered interactive cards
--shadow-4dialogs, sheets, the mobile navigation

In dark mode shadows become deeper and black. Surfaces also get lighter as they rise, so depth stays visible without glow.

Usage

  • Never invent a z-index. Pick a layer, and prefer the browser top layer (<dialog>, the Popover API) for overlays.
  • Hover lifts move cards up by --lift (3px) and raise the shadow one step. Under reduced motion they stay put; the shadow change alone is enough.
  • Do not stack shadows on nested surfaces. A card inside a dialog uses a hairline.
